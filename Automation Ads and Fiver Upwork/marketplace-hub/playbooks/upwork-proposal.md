# Playbook: Upwork proposals

**Status: live.** Written from `research/marketplace-expansion.md` sections 1 and 2, which carry the URLs for every figure below. Nothing here is inferred from how Fiverr works. Upwork is a different machine.

The one-sentence version: **filter hard, write few, write well, send fast, price high, always offer the Loom.**

---

## 1. Why Upwork, and what it actually pays

Upwork publishes buyer-facing price guidance on each `/hire/` page. That is the number in the client's head before they post anything, so it is the ceiling we can quote without looking mad.

| Engagement, Upwork's own guidance | Range |
|---|---|
| Single-flow voice agent | $600 to $6,000 |
| Multi-intent voice assistant | $1,200 to $12,000 |
| **Production-grade deployment** | **$2,400 to $24,000** |
| Enterprise multi-agent system | $4,800 to $48,000 |
| Voice / IVR systems | $5,000 to $12,000 |
| Workflow automation agent | $2,000 to $5,000 |
| n8n custom integrations with error handling | $800 to $2,500 |
| AI governance framework | $1,500 to $4,000 |
| Full-funnel appointment setting | $2,000 to $5,000 |

Hourly guidance on the voice and agent pages is **$30 to $150/hr**.

**And the posted reality.** Across 50,019 fixed-price postings with listed budgets in June 2026: median posted budget **$100**, median effective hourly rate about **$8.75**, 31.5% of postings under $50, only **12.5% at $1,000 or more**. A further 49,099 postings were hourly. **24.0% of postings come from clients with unverified payment.** A $10 Retell job and a $2,000 enterprise agent job sit in the same category, which is why the entire skill on this platform is filtering.

Our floor, stated so it is not renegotiated in the moment: **$1,200 on any Upwork build contract.** A job posted at $150 is not a discount opportunity, it is a different market. Walk.

## 2. Connects: what a proposal costs

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Price per Connect | **$0.15** |
| Free monthly Connects, Basic plan | **10** |
| One-time new-freelancer bonus | 50 Connects, **only after** purchasing Connects or subscribing to Freelancer Plus |
| Basic or entry job | 4 to 6 Connects, $0.60 to $0.90 |
| Standard job | 8 to 10 Connects, $1.20 to $1.50 |
| Large budget | 12 to 14 Connects, $1.80 to $2.10 |
| Enterprise or featured | 14 to 16 Connects, $2.10 to $2.40 |

Connects are refunded **only** when the client cancels the job before any contract forms, or Upwork removes the post for a Terms of Service violation. They are **not** refunded for a rejected proposal, a client hiring someone else, an expired job or a withdrawn proposal.

Cost per acquired client at 8 to 10 Connects per proposal: 2% win rate is $60, 5% is $24, 10% is $12, 20% is $6.

**Read that honestly. At a $3,500 first engagement, a 2% win rate costs $60 of Connects against $3,500 of revenue. Connects are not the constraint. Proposal-writing time is.** That single fact is why this playbook says write few and write well rather than bid more.

## 3. Boosted proposals

Mechanics, from Upwork's own help article:

- **Four boosted slots** at the top of the client's proposal list, each with a "Boosted" icon.
- You "can bid any Connects amount". No published minimum or maximum.
- The auction settles on **your own bid**, not the runner-up's. Upwork's worked example: "You bid 35 Connects... The other winning bidders bid 30, 25, and 20 Connects. You'll pay the exact amount of your bid of 35 Connects."
- You pay only if you are in the top four when the auction closes, or the client interacts with your proposal. Otherwise the boost Connects are refunded.
- **Only the boost Connects are refundable. The standard Connects used to submit are not.**
- The boost holds until the client interacts, opens it three times without action, sees it five times without interaction, or you are outbid.

Third-party data: the auction runs 7 days or until a hire; Upwork claims boosted proposals are 17% more likely to be seen and can raise hire probability by up to 24%; recommended practice is to boost 20% to 30% of proposals strategically and keep boost spend under 2% of expected contract value.

**Our rule, written before the first boost rather than rationalised after it:** boost only jobs priced **$1,500+** where we have a named vertical proof, cap the boost at **30 Connects ($4.50)**, boost at most 25% of proposals, and never boost a job we would not personally want. Kill the boost habit entirely if boosted interviews do not exceed organic interviews over 20 boosted proposals.

## 4. The first two lines are the whole competition

A client sees a list of proposals collapsed to the first two lines. Everything below them only matters if they win.

**This is the highest-value data in the entire research pack, because the effect sizes are measured across 133,872 proposals rather than asserted. Baseline reply rate: 7.45%.**

**AI cliché density is a kill switch:**

| Clichés in the cover letter | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Zero | **7.55%** |
| One | 6.84% |
| Three | 4.17% |
| **Four or more** | **0.00%** |

Zero is not a target. It is a pass/fail gate.

**Worst opening lines, as percentage-point drag against the 7.45% baseline:**

| Opening | Drag |
|---|---|
| "Hi! Thanks for posting" | **-5.50pp** |
| "Many freelancers..." | -4.03pp |
| "Good day" | -3.68pp |
| "I have experience with" | -3.67pp |
| "I'll help" | -3.17pp |

**Elements that lift reply rate:**

| Element | Lift |
|---|---|
| **Offer a Loom walkthrough** | **+3.70pp** |
| Reference recently completed work | +2.52pp |
| Open with a specific question | +2.34pp |
| The word "guarantee" | part of the winning combination |
| "any questions" as a low-friction close | part of the winning combination |

**Proposals combining those signals replied at 13.77%, nearly double the platform mean.**

Two more measured findings worth stating plainly. Hand-written templates reply at **8.13%**, GPT-4o auto-bidders at **7.13%**, newer LLM stacks at **6.91%**: **the AI-written proposal is measurably worse than a human template.** And defensive disclaimers backfire, with proposals containing "non-ChatGPT generated proposal" replying at only 5.9%.

Length: **150 to 250 words.** Longer is not more convincing, it is less read.

## 5. Speed, price position and client type

**Speed.** Under 5 minutes from post to proposal replies at **8.99%**, 5 to 10 minutes at 6.91%, 12 to 15 minutes at 8.07%. Premium opportunities attract **40+ proposals within 2 hours**, and practitioners report 20 to 50 proposals on essentially every beginner-to-intermediate post. Average time from job post to first proposal, per Upwork, is three hours, which is the gap we exploit by bidding in the first five minutes.

**Price position, the counterintuitive one:**

| Bid as % of posted budget | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Under 50% | 20.6% |
| **200% to 500%** | **16.4%** |
| 95% to 105%, matching the budget | **8.8%, the worst position on the board** |

Matching the budget signals a commodity supplier. Bidding 3x **with a reason attached** signals a different category of vendor. We never bid under 50%, despite its headline rate, because that is the race we are structurally trying not to be in and it anchors us permanently.

**Client type:**

| Client lifetime spend | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| $1,000 to $5,000 | **8.15%, the highest** |
| $0, brand new | 6.89% |
| $500,000+ whales | 3.85%, the lowest |

Target the $1,000 to $5,000 band. They have paid before, they are not procurement-heavy, and they reply most.

## 7. Hourly versus fixed price

**Fixed-price protection requires three things,** quoted from Upwork: "The milestone was fully funded before you began working"; "You used the Submit Work for Payment button to deliver your work"; "The submitted work matches the milestone description". Hourly protection is a separate scheme keyed to time logged through the Upwork desktop app with work-diary screenshots.

**Our rule, without hedging: fixed price with milestones, always, for build work.** Three reasons. Our delivery is a productized one-session clone process, so logged hours misrepresent the value. JSS weights contract value, and a fixed $3,500 contract reads better than 40 logged hours at $87. And the work-diary requirement is incompatible with how the build actually happens.

Use hourly **only** for open-ended tuning retainers after launch, where the recurring-client JSS compounding is the real prize.

Fees: the freelancer service fee is variable, **0% to 15% per contract**, locked at the moment the proposal is sent, with a blended agency average of 11% to 13.5%. All-in effective take rate including Connects burn and proposal labour runs 22% to 34% for typical agencies and 14% to 18% for well-filtered ones, against Fiverr's flat 20%. **Upwork is cheaper than Fiverr on fees if and only if we filter hard.**

## 8. Rising Talent and JSS mechanics

**Rising Talent**, quoted from Upwork. Either an invitation, or all of:

- "an average star rating of 4.8 or above and earned at least $250 USD in the past 12 months"
- "Maintain a 100% complete profile with accurate skills and genuine work"
- "Have submitted a proposal or worked within the past 90 days"
- "Have a Job Success Score (JSS) of 90% or higher, if you've earned one"
- No account holds, no public or private negative feedback
- Identification verified badge and a valid withdrawal method

**One $450 order clears it.** Compare Fiverr's inbound Briefs channel, locked behind Success Score 7, which needs 20 orders from 10 unique clients. Rising Talent is reachable in week two.

**Top Rated:** JSS 90%+, first project more than 90 days ago, $1,000 of 12-month earnings, 100% complete profile, and Rising Talent or JSS 90%+ for 13 of the last 16 weeks. **The money bar clears on order one. The binding constraint is the 90-day clock, which is why the account opens on day 1 even if we do not bid hard until week 3.**

**Job Success Score mechanics:**

- (successful outcomes minus negative outcomes) ÷ total outcomes.
- **Does not display until 10 billable hours or one completed fixed-price contract.**
- Recalculated daily in the background, **visible profile update every 14 days**. Half of your weekly reads will be unchanged by design.
- Three simultaneous windows at 6, 12 and 24 months, and **the highest of the three is displayed**, which creates a forgiveness curve: a bad outcome drops out of the 6-month window by about month 7.
- **Private feedback, 1 to 10, is weighted as heavily as the public star rating.** A 5-star public rating with a 6 in private feedback still drags JSS down.
- Recurring clients paying every 90 days weigh **very high** and compound as repeated successful outcomes.
- **Freelancer-initiated cancellations are a very strong negative.**
- Below JSS 75, "response rates collapse".

Upwork's search algorithm is reported to weight JSS at 25% to 30%, relevance 20% to 25%, availability and response metrics 15% to 20%, earnings history 10% to 15%, profile completeness 10% to 15%, reviews 10% to 15%. Setting status to "Not Available" reportedly costs a 50% to 70% visibility reduction.

**Two operating rules follow, and they are not negotiable.** Never cancel a contract ourselves: negotiate a reduced-scope close instead. And attach a monitoring retainer to every build, because a client paying every 90 days is worth more to JSS than its revenue.

## 9. The Loom is not optional

It is the single largest measured lift available at **+3.70pp**, roughly a 50% relative increase on the 7.45% baseline, and it is also the only way to demonstrate a voice product in a text medium.

- **60 to 90 seconds.** Address the client by name.
- Show a **live call with the latency readout visible on screen**.
- One vertical-specific Loom per vertical beats one generic Loom, because specificity is where the lift comes from.
- Re-record after every delivery, so the Loom always shows the most recent build.
- Never show or speak a dialed number. Mask it in every frame.

The raw material already exists: dashboard screenshots with the latency waterfall, the compliance panel, and a live demo line.

## 10. Operating rules

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Log every proposal | `--entity proposal --action sent` with the job ref, the Connects spent, the bid, and the posted budget. Without it the interview rate is unknowable |
| Log every view, reply and interview | `--action viewed`, `--action replied`, `--action interview` |
| 3 to 5 proposals a day, not 20 | 3 to 5 strong-fit jobs beats 20 random ones. Ramp from 5 to 10 in week 2 to 10 to 15 in week 3 |
| Cliché count checked before every send | Four or more replies at 0.00%. This is a pass/fail gate, checked, not assumed |
| Under 250 words, every time | Measured. Longer is less read |
| Time-to-proposal recorded per proposal | Under 5 minutes replies at 8.99%. If we cannot measure it we cannot improve it |
| Bid as a percentage of posted budget recorded per proposal | 200% to 500% replies at 16.4%, matching replies at 8.8% |
| Never reuse a proposal verbatim | The hook is the proposal, and a hook cannot be generic |
| Never let an AI write the final text | AI-written proposals reply at 6.91% to 7.13% against 8.13% for hand-written templates. Research and draft, then rewrite by hand |
| Never add a defensive disclaimer | "non-ChatGPT generated proposal" replies at 5.9% |
| Never cancel a contract ourselves | Very strong JSS negative |
| Set the cost-per-interview ceiling before boosting | Not after |

## 11. The 14-day decision rule

The only scheduled stop-and-rewrite point in the month.

- **Interview rate above 7% and no hire yet: change nothing.** 20 to 80 serious proposals is the documented range to a first client. Changing the template at proposal 25 destroys the only sample we have.
- **Interview rate below 5% after 20 proposals: stop bidding for one full day.** Rewrite the two opening lines, record a new Loom, resume. Do not also change volume.
- **Proposals not being opened at all: the problem is upstream.** Profile headline, specialized-profile choice, or badge state. Fixing the cover letter cannot fix a profile problem.

Planning number to first hire: **30 to 50 high-signal proposals over 3 to 5 weeks, at roughly 10 Connects each, so $45 to $75 of Connects.** We sit in the "strong portfolio, clear niche" bucket, which benchmarks at 1 to 3 weeks to first client, **if and only if the portfolio is populated on day one**.

## 12. Voice

Confident engineer-lawyer. No hype, no superlatives, no emoji, no exclamation marks.

- Every claim carries its measurement basis and the date it was read.
- One number, not a brochure of them.
- A target is never quoted as a result. Answer rate, booking rate and calls-at-scale are not measured and are never claimed.
- Limitations stated before the client finds them.
- Compliance framing is engineering, not legal advice. Never imply US bar admission. Never imply insurance licensure.
- **No em dashes.** Split the sentence, use a colon, or use a comma.
