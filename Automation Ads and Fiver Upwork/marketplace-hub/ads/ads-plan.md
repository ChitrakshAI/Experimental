# Ads plan

**Status: live.** Every mechanic and every benchmark below is verified in `research/marketplace-expansion.md` section 4, which carries the URLs. The numbers that are still genuinely unknown are marked and they stay marked: an invented CPM is worse than an empty cell, because an empty cell cannot be budgeted against by mistake.

Founder directive being executed here, quoted: **"GLOBAL positioning, no limits, maximum results, ads across native boosts of Upwork, Fiverr, Meta etc, perform on the budget and get clients."**

The way to execute that literally is section 4, three tiers plus a no-limits tier, and the way to execute it *well* is section 5, the measurement contract, because "perform on the budget" is a performance instruction and the fastest way to fail it is to scale an unmeasured channel.

---

## 0. The ordering rule

**Spend the platform-native budget first.** Connects, boosts, Seller Plus Kickstart, the Availability Badge. It is measured in tens of dollars and it converts against buyers who are already in-market with a stated need. Only then buy external traffic.

**Point external traffic at the gig URL, not at a landing page**, during the honeymoon window. Two reasons, and the second is the important one:

1. The gig URL is the only landing page whose conversion the ledger can measure.
2. An order bought with ad money is a **permanent ranking deposit**. "When someone from LinkedIn, Reddit, or your personal network clicks your Fiverr gig link and places an order, that interaction looks identical to Fiverr's algorithm to an order that came through organic search." A landing-page lead is not.

**The honeymoon window.** New gigs get elevated visibility for **48 hours to several weeks** as a data-collection phase. The counterweight, from the same body of reporting: "Fiverr's algorithm is not your sales team during the first 60 to 90 days." Both are true, and together they define the launch exactly: the algorithm gives a short window of free impressions, and whether we convert them sets our ranking for months. **External traffic during that window is the single highest-leverage launch action available.**

## 1. Fiverr Promoted Gigs

**Model.** A cost-per-click auction. Fiverr: "Our auction system will calculate the minimum amount per click you need to pay to win over other ads." You "Control your daily budget" and "Your ads will be promoted until your budget runs out for the day." Payment is retrospective: "At the end of every month, we'll withdraw payment for your ad activity directly from your Fiverr earnings, no upfront payments needed."

**Eligibility, and the thing that changes the plan.** Fiverr does not publish the gate on its own page. Third-party reporting puts it at Level 1 and above with a gig carrying at least 20 completed orders in some categories, with new gigs ineligible until they have conversion data; a second source says Level 2 grants full access. **The two sources disagree, and both of them put it out of reach for us in the first 30 days.**

**Operational consequence, stated plainly: Promoted Gigs is unavailable to us in month 1. Any budget plan that assumes it is wrong.** Budget for it in month 2 or 3. Verify the real rule inside the seller dashboard and log the answer as a ledger event before planning a dollar of spend.

**Costs when it opens.** Typical CPCs run **$0.10 to $2.00**. Recommended test: **$3 to $5 a day for two to three weeks**. The ROI rule: positive if cost per order stays below 20% of net earnings per order. The maths works best for high-priced gigs with 8%+ organic conversion and fails entirely for gigs under $30.

**Our arithmetic.** At a $450 Starter package netting $360 after Fiverr's 20%, the ceiling is **$72 per order**. At $0.50 CPC and 5% conversion the real cost is about **$10 per order**. Promoted Gigs should be extremely profitable for us once eligible, **precisely because our anchor is high**. That is one more argument against ever launching a cheap gig.

**Creative.** There is none to make. Promoted Gigs shows the live listing, which means the gig image and the price ladder *are* the ad, and improving the ad means improving the listing.

**Sequencing rule.** Never promote two gigs in the same week during launch. Two variables moving at once produces a result nobody can attribute.

**Kill rule.** Pause if seven days of spend produce clicks and zero messages, or if cost per message exceeds 10% of the Basic package price on that gig.

## 2. Fiverr Seller Plus Kickstart

**$15/month**, "exclusively for New freelancers who haven't yet received a Fiverr level", refundable if cancelled "within 48 hours of your last monthly payment".

What it includes, quoted: performance dashboard with **"Keyword research and pricing insights"**; "actionable, AI-powered suggestions to improve your Gigs"; client-behaviour insights; promotions and coupons; a "Live walkthrough session with a Fiverr onboarding specialist"; **"Verify your identity easily to build client trust"**; faster customer support response.

**Seller Plus buys no ranking.** Free and paid sellers are treated equally by the search algorithm. Nobody should expect a boost and nobody should report one.

**Buy it for exactly one reason:** the keyword data settles the tool-keyword versus vertical-keyword question **empirically, within weeks**, and that is the single largest open strategic question we have. Identity verification is a second, smaller reason. Read the keyword data on day 10 and act on it: if tool keywords dominate, swap one tag on Gig 1 toward tool language; if vertical keywords dominate, the positioning is confirmed and nothing changes.

## 4. Off-platform: Meta, Google, LinkedIn

### Is driving external traffic to a Fiverr gig allowed?

**Yes, explicitly and enthusiastically.** From Fiverr's own "Promoting your Gigs outside of Fiverr":

> **"Sharing a link to your Gig or profile is encouraged."**

Permitted: dedicated social business accounts, personal websites and blogs, forums and communities, **paid advertising on Facebook, Instagram and Google**, guest posts, podcast appearances.

Prohibited: sharing personal contact information in external promotion, moving communication off-platform (all communication must happen on the platform), fake reviews or fabricated testimonials, AI-generated misleading visuals or deepfakes, impersonation.

**The comparison that settles the order.** Meta B2B at $2.52 CPC is roughly half Google Business Services at $5.87 and a third of LinkedIn at $5 to $8. **Automotive search at $2.27 CPC and $44.26 CPL is the cheapest qualified traffic in the whole table and it maps exactly onto our strongest vertical.**

And the exact-match query "ai receptionist for car dealership" is a low-volume, high-intent, low-competition four-word phrase in a category where the Fiverr `car-dealership` keyword carries 2,699 services and zero AI voice gigs. LinkedIn's reported average conversion rate is 6.1% against 3.75% for Google Search, which is why it earns a slot at the $3,000 tier despite the highest CPC on the list.

### Hard creative constraints

- **Never print or speak a phone number, email or any external contact detail** in an ad that lands on a Fiverr gig, and never in the gig itself. Personal contact details in gigs are an explicit denial trigger. The demo line is delivered inside the order or shown in the gig video.
- Never move the conversation off-platform once it starts on the marketplace.
- PII-masked dashboard views only. No client, dealership or agency named.
- Every printed figure carries its measurement basis and the date it was read.
- Label AI-generated media where platform policy requires it. No misleading synthetic visuals, no deepfakes.
- Compliance content is framed as engineering, not legal advice. Never imply US bar admission or insurance licensure.
- **No em dashes** in any headline, body, caption or text baked into an image.

## 6. The measurement contract

**No channel gets more money until it has a measured baseline conversion rate at its current budget.** Six gates, in order, all of them checkable from the ledger:

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **G1. Baseline exists** | 7 consecutive days at the current budget, with spend and clicks logged daily through `hub.py ads log` against a registered campaign id |
| **G2. Funnel measured end to end** | Clicks, then messages or leads, then orders, with real numbers. Clicks with no downstream count is an unmeasured channel whatever the click volume |
| **G3. Cost per message is known** | Cost per message, or cost per interview on Upwork, is a number and not an estimate. Until then there is no denominator and no decision |
| **G4. It beats the alternative** | Its cost per message is at or below the cheapest currently-running channel, or it is the only channel reaching that audience and we write that down |
| **G5. Kill rule written first** | The campaign record carries a kill rule with a number in it, entered **before** the spend |
| **G6. One variable at a time** | Never two gigs promoted in the same week during launch, and never a budget change plus a creative change on the same campaign in the same week |

**Increment size: at most 2x per week on a proven line.** From $135 that reaches $2,160 in five weeks, which is faster than we can generate the case studies to support the volume anyway.

**Kill outright rather than halving.** A half-funded ad group produces half the signal at full attention cost.

### The command

Every campaign writes into the same ledger as everything else. There is no separate ads spreadsheet, because a separate spreadsheet is how spend stops being comparable to outcomes.

```bash
# the campaign must exist in ads/campaigns.json first
python3 hub.py ads list

python3 hub.py ads log --campaign gads-search-intent --date 2026-10-02 \
  --spend 4.80 --clicks 2 --impressions 61 \
  --note "day 1, dealership ad group only"
```

That writes one row to `ledger/metrics.jsonl` and one event to `ledger/events.jsonl`. `hub.py metrics report` then rolls ad spend and ad clicks into the same weekly table as impressions, messages and orders, so cost per message is readable without joining anything by hand. Paid impressions are stored separately from organic ones, because summing them would quietly inflate the CTR denominator and make a bought impression look like a won one.

**The flag that matters most:** ad spend greater than zero with zero ad clicks fires automatically. Pause first, diagnose second.

## 7. Campaign naming convention

`<channel>-<target>-<variant>`, all lowercase, hyphen separated, no spaces, stable for the life of the campaign.

| Segment | Values |
|---|---|
| channel | `fvr` Fiverr, `upw` Upwork, `meta`, `gads` Google, `li` LinkedIn |
| target | the gig ref (`gig1`), the vertical (`dealers`, `agencies`, `insurance`), or the mechanism (`connects`, `boost`, `kickstart`, `availability`) |
| variant | optional: `us`, `retarget`, `default`, `test-b` |

Examples: `fvr-promoted-gig1`, `upw-boost-default`, `meta-dealers-us`, `gads-search-intent`, `li-insurance-us`, `meta-dealers-us-retarget`.

**Rules.** The id is permanent once spend has been logged against it, because renaming breaks the series. A creative change inside the same audience and budget keeps the id and gets a ledger event. A new audience or a new objective gets a new id. Never reuse a retired id.
